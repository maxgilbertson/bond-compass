# Bond briefing: week to Friday 13 November 2026

## In brief

- Government bond yields rose almost everywhere this week, so bond prices fell. The US 10-year yield rose 28 basis points to 5.14%, the UK 10-year 19 basis points to 5.35%, and Japan's 23 basis points to 2.87%.
- Most of this week's rise only undid recent falls: over a month, the UK, German, Italian and Japanese 10-year yields are all slightly lower.
- Company bonds held up well. The extra yield on riskier company bonds fell sharply, by 22 basis points in the US and 26 in Europe.
- The US 10-year yield after expected inflation (2.86%) is higher than on any day since 2016.
- The top-scored practice portfolio is worth £10,244 against a £10,000 start, the lowest-scored £9,906. Six weeks of results mean very little.

## Yields this week

A basis point is 0.01 percentage points, so 28 basis points (bp) is 0.28 percentage points. When yields rise, the prices of existing bonds fall, and longer-dated bonds fall most.

| 10-year yield | Level | Week | Month | Hedged into pounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | 5.35% | up 19bp | down 2bp | 5.35% |
| United States | 5.14% | up 28bp | up 5bp | 5.26% |
| France | 4.92% | up 16bp | up 14bp | 6.43% |
| Italy | 4.43% | up 9bp | down 6bp | 5.93% |
| Germany | 3.38% | up 12bp | down 3bp | 4.88% |
| Japan | 2.87% | up 23bp | down 6bp | 5.62% |

"Hedged into pounds" is roughly what a UK investor would earn after removing the currency risk, which is the fair way to compare countries. On that basis France (6.43%) and Italy (5.93%) pay more than UK gilts (5.35%), and Japan's low headline yield becomes 5.62%.

In the US, the 10-year yield is 29bp above the 2-year yield, a gap that narrowed by 5bp this week. That means short-term US yields rose slightly more than long-term ones.

In the euro area, France pays 155bp more than Germany for 10-year money, and Italy 105bp more. France has been the weaker of the two over the month: its yield is up 14bp while Italy's is down 6bp.

## Biggest moves

The largest rises were spread across very different markets. Turkey's 10-year yield rose 45bp to 33.52%. Note that once the currency risk is removed, a UK investor would keep only about 1.52% of that. Hong Kong rose 33bp to 3.99%, and the US 28bp. Singapore (up 24bp to 2.33%) and South Africa (up 24bp to 8.68%) followed.

The falls were mostly in Asian and frontier markets, where bond holders gained. Nigeria's 10-year yield fell 25bp to 16.60%, the Philippines' 17bp to 7.75%, and Indonesia's 9bp to 7.61%. Egypt's 1-year yield dipped 8bp, and Thailand was unchanged at 1.99%.

When yields rise across most countries at once, it usually reflects shifting expectations for interest rates or inflation rather than news about one government.

## Credit, inflation and volatility

The "spread" is the extra yield a company bond pays over a government bond. A falling spread means investors are less worried about companies failing to pay.

- **US investment grade** (the safest companies): 89bp, down 4bp. That is higher than on 60% of days since 2023, so slightly above the middle of its range.
- **US high yield** (riskier companies): 320bp, down 22bp. Higher than on 71% of days since 2023, so still a little wide, but less so than a week ago.
- **Euro high yield**: 323bp, down 26bp. Higher than on 57% of days since 2023.
- **Emerging-market companies**: 167bp, down 15bp. Higher than on 45% of days since 2023, close to the middle.

On inflation, markets expect US inflation of 2.24% a year over the next ten years (up 1bp), and 2.18% for the five years starting five years from now (up 1bp). Both are close to normal; the Federal Reserve targets 2%. The US 10-year yield after expected inflation, called the real yield, rose 23bp to 2.86%. That is higher than on every day since 2016, so most of the week's US rise came from real yields rather than inflation worries.

The MOVE index, which measures how much traders expect US government bond yields to swing, is 99, down 4 over the month. That is normal: below 80 counts as calm, and 120 or above as stressed.

## Central banks

No central bank followed here changed interest rates this week. Over the past three months, 13 of the 26 central banks followed raised rates and 4 cut them. Rate rises tend to push short-term yields up most.

Next week there are no scheduled rate decisions in the data, but UK inflation figures for October (CPI) are due from the ONS on Wednesday 18 November.

## Scores and signals

Scores rank markets against each other. The highest are Indonesia (68), the Philippines (63), and Brazil, France and Poland (62 each), all currently marked Overweight, meaning the numbers favour holding more. Poland's price was not updated this week, so treat its figures with care. The lowest are China (17, marked Avoid), Thailand (26), Singapore (27), Hungary (28) and Japan (36).

The biggest score rises were the US (up 3.1 points to 51), Canada (up 2.9 to 50) and Belgium (up 2.5 to 57). The biggest fall was Spain, down 7.4 points to 45, followed by Norway (down 3.6 to 55) and Austria (down 3.2 to 42).

Signals flickered for two markets sitting on a boundary. Hong Kong moved between Underweight and Neutral three times and ended the week at Neutral. Chile dropped from Overweight to Neutral on 11 November and returned to Overweight the next day. Back-and-forth changes like these usually mean a score is sitting right at a cut-off, not that anything important has changed.
