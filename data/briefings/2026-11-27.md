# Bond briefing: week to Friday 27 November 2026

## In brief

- Government bond yields fell in five of the six main markets this week, so bond prices rose there. The UK 10-year yield fell 6 basis points to 5.18%. The US was the exception, up 2 basis points to 4.94%.
- France and Japan had the largest falls among the main markets, down 16 and 15 basis points. France now pays 144 basis points more than Germany for 10-year money.
- Brazil's 10-year yield fell 130 basis points to 14.36%, a large gain for holders. Its score then dropped 24.6 points to 37, and its signal went from Overweight to Underweight.
- Riskier US company bonds pay 377 basis points more than US government bonds, wider than on 96% of days since 2023. The site still marks credit as under stress.
- The top-scored practice portfolio is worth £10,352 against a £10,000 start, the lowest-scored £9,996. Less than two months of results mean very little.

## Yields this week

A basis point is 0.01 percentage points, so 16 basis points (bp) is 0.16 percentage points. When yields fall, the prices of existing bonds rise, and longer-dated bonds rise most. When yields rise, the opposite happens.

| 10-year yield | Level | Week | Month | Hedged into pounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | 5.18% | down 6bp | down 12bp | 5.18% |
| United States | 4.94% | up 2bp | down 5bp | 5.06% |
| France | 4.71% | down 16bp | down 17bp | 6.21% |
| Italy | 4.32% | down 9bp | down 14bp | 5.82% |
| Germany | 3.27% | down 3bp | down 7bp | 4.77% |
| Japan | 2.64% | down 15bp | down 20bp | 5.39% |

"Hedged into pounds" is roughly what a UK investor would earn after removing the currency risk, which is the fair way to compare countries. On that basis France (6.21%), Italy (5.82%) and Japan (5.39%) pay more than UK gilts (5.18%), and Germany (4.77%) pays less.

In the US, the 10-year yield is 23bp above the 2-year yield. That gap narrowed by 7bp this week, which means short-term US yields rose more than long-term ones.

In the euro area, France pays 144bp more than Germany for 10-year money, and Italy 105bp more. French and Italian yields both fell further than Germany's this week, so both gaps narrowed.

## Biggest moves

The largest fall was in Brazil, where the 10-year yield dropped 130bp to 14.36%. Holders of Brazilian bonds gained. Once the currency risk is removed, that 14.36% becomes about 4.61% for a UK investor. After France and Japan, Greece fell 13bp to 4.13% and Australia 10bp to 4.85%.

The largest rise was in Turkey, where the 10-year yield rose 142bp to 34.97%, so bond prices there fell. It is up 194bp over a month. Hedged into pounds, that yield is only about 2.97%. The other rises were small: India up 8bp to 7.27%, and Indonesia, Switzerland and Egypt's 1-year yield up about 3bp each.

The facts do not say why any of these markets moved.

## Credit, inflation and volatility

The "spread" is the extra yield a company bond pays over a government bond. A wider spread means investors want more reward for the risk of companies failing to pay. All four measures edged up this week.

- **US investment grade** (the safest companies): 99bp, up 1bp.
- **US high yield** (riskier companies): 377bp, up 1bp on the week and 51bp over a month. Higher than on 96% of days since 2023, which keeps the site's credit stress signal switched on.
- **Euro high yield**: 359bp, up 5bp.
- **Emerging-market companies**: 202bp, up 4bp.

On inflation, markets expect US inflation of 2.15% a year over the next ten years (unchanged on the week, down 20bp over a month), and 2.14% for the five years starting five years from now (up 2bp). Both are close to normal; the Federal Reserve targets 2%. The US 10-year yield after expected inflation, called the real yield, fell 4bp to 2.73%. That is still higher than on 99% of days since 2016.

The MOVE index, which measures how much traders expect US government bond yields to swing, is 95, up 1 over the month. That is normal: 80 or below counts as calm, and 120 or above as stressed.

## Central banks

No central bank followed here changed rates this week. Over the past three months, 15 of the 26 raised rates and 3 cut them. Rate rises push short-term yields up most. Two data releases are due next week: the first estimate of euro-area inflation for November on Tuesday 1 December, and the US jobs report for November on Friday 4 December.

## Scores and signals

Scores rank the 33 markets against each other. The highest are Indonesia (69), Chile (66), the Philippines (63), and Peru and New Zealand (62 each). All five are marked Overweight, meaning the numbers favour holding more, which makes them worth a closer look and nothing more. The lowest are China (17, marked Avoid), Singapore (25), Thailand (26), Hungary (28) and Brazil (37).

Brazil had by far the biggest change, down 24.6 points to 37. Austria fell 5.7 points to 46. The biggest rise was the US, up 5.9 points to 50, followed by South Korea (up 3.5 to 49), Finland (up 3.4 to 54) and the UK (up 3.1 to 55).

One signal change lasted: Brazil moved from Overweight to Neutral on 27 November and to Underweight, meaning the numbers favour holding less, a day later. Four others reversed within days. New Zealand and Peru dropped to Neutral and returned to Overweight. India and France rose to Overweight and went back to Neutral. Back-and-forth changes like these usually mean a score is sitting right at a cut-off.
