# Bond briefing: week to Friday 20 November 2026

## In brief

- Government bond yields fell in all six main markets this week, so bond prices rose. The US 10-year yield fell 20 basis points to 4.95% and the UK 10-year 10 basis points to 5.24%.
- Company bonds went the other way. The extra yield on riskier US company bonds rose 29 basis points to 352, wider than on 92% of days since 2023.
- Yields rose in several emerging markets, led by Brazil, where the 10-year yield rose 33 basis points to 15.66%. Bond holders there lost ground.
- Chile's score jumped 8.5 points to 69, joint highest with Indonesia. The US score fell 7.4 points to 45.
- The top-scored practice portfolio is worth £10,225 against a £10,000 start, the lowest-scored £9,944. Less than two months of results mean very little.

## Yields this week

A basis point is 0.01 percentage points, so 20 basis points (bp) is 0.20 percentage points. When yields fall, the prices of existing bonds rise, and longer-dated bonds rise most. When yields rise, the opposite happens.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | 5.24% | down 10bp | down 9bp | 5.24% |
| United States | 4.95% | down 20bp | unchanged | 5.08% |
| France | 4.87% | down 5bp | down 22bp | 6.37% |
| Italy | 4.41% | down 2bp | down 21bp | 5.91% |
| Germany | 3.29% | down 9bp | down 5bp | 4.79% |
| Japan | 2.79% | down 8bp | down 10bp | 5.54% |

"Hedged into pounds" is roughly what a UK investor would earn after removing the currency risk, which is the fair way to compare countries. On that basis France (6.37%) and Italy (5.91%) pay more than UK gilts (5.24%), and Germany (4.79%) pays less.

In the US, the 10-year yield is 30bp above the 2-year yield, a gap that widened by 2bp this week. That means short-term US yields fell slightly more than long-term ones.

In the euro area, France pays 158bp more than Germany for 10-year money, and Italy 112bp more. Both French and Italian yields fell less than Germany's this week, although over a month they have fallen much further (22bp and 21bp, against Germany's 5bp).

## Biggest moves

The largest falls in yield, where bond holders gained most, were in developed markets. After the US (down 20bp), Australia's 10-year yield fell 19bp to 4.95% and Canada's 15bp to 3.71%. India (down 12bp to 7.19%) and South Korea (down 12bp to 3.87%) followed.

The rises were all in emerging markets, where bond prices fell. Brazil's 10-year yield rose 33bp to 15.66%, and is up 264bp over a month. Note that once the currency risk is removed, that 15.66% becomes about 5.91% for a UK investor. Peru rose 19bp to 6.78%, South Africa 17bp, and Indonesia 10bp to 7.71%. Chile also rose 10bp.

The facts do not say why. Falling yields in safer markets alongside rising yields in riskier ones usually reflect investors becoming more cautious.

## Credit, inflation and volatility

The "spread" is the extra yield a company bond pays over a government bond. A rising spread means investors want more reward for the risk of companies failing to pay. All four measures rose this week.

- **US investment grade** (the safest companies): 94bp, up 5bp. Higher than on 77% of days since 2023.
- **US high yield** (riskier companies): 352bp, up 29bp. Higher than on 92% of days since 2023, which is wide enough for the site to mark credit as under stress.
- **Euro high yield**: 337bp, up 11bp. Higher than on 69% of days since 2023.
- **Emerging-market companies**: 184bp, up 15bp. Higher than on 68% of days since 2023.

On inflation, markets expect US inflation of 2.15% a year over the next ten years (down 9bp), and 2.12% for the five years starting five years from now (down 6bp). Both are close to normal; the Federal Reserve targets 2%. The US 10-year yield after expected inflation, called the real yield, fell 3bp to 2.88%. That is still higher than on 99% of days since 2016.

The MOVE index, which measures how much traders expect US government bond yields to swing, is 106, up 10 over the month. That is normal: 80 or below counts as calm, and 120 or above as stressed.

## Scores and signals

Scores rank the 33 markets against each other. The highest are Chile and Indonesia (69 each), then Brazil, Mexico and Poland (62 each). All five are marked Overweight, meaning the numbers favour holding more, which makes them worth a closer look and nothing more. Poland's price was not updated this week, so treat its figures with care. The lowest are China (18, marked Avoid), Thailand (25), Singapore (27), Hungary (28) and Japan (38).

Chile had by far the biggest rise, up 8.5 points to 69. Hong Kong rose 3.4 points to 45, and India and the Netherlands 2.5 points each, to 57 and 48. The biggest fall was the US, down 7.4 points to 45, followed by Canada (down 3.7 to 46) and Australia (down 2.8 to 51). Those are also the three markets where yields fell most.

One signal change lasted: France moved from Overweight to Neutral on 17 November. Three others reversed within days. Peru dropped to Neutral and returned to Overweight, New Zealand did the same, and the UK rose to Overweight for a day before going back to Neutral. Back-and-forth changes like these usually mean a score is sitting right at a cut-off.
